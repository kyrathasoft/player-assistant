Here is a technical answer:

I’m building the English-to-Orcish dictionary/translator as a structured lexicon rather than a flat word list: each entry preserves part of speech, sense, register, grammar tags, plural/possessive forms, and usage nuance so the translator can choose context-aware matches instead of collapsing unrelated meanings into one token. New terms should extend existing roots, compounds, affixes, and inflection patterns when possible; fixed phrases or compounds get explicit entries when composition would lose meaning. Before adding vocabulary, I check for exact and near collisions with existing terms and affix patterns, keep noun/verb/adjective distinctions separate, and verify representative translations so that the dictionary stays internally consistent, reversible where intended, and useful for both direct lookup and natural-sounding Orcish output.

I'm using Chat-GPT-5.5-Pro to help guide lexicon formation. The translator will eventually be able to convert Issenda Orcish to English, as well.
