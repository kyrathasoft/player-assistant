Ch 3 - Joining the Caravan to Raven's Pass

They still didn’t know the full strength of the orcspawn, nor the truth behind the other horn call. Racing to Raven’s Pass with little more than rumours and half seen threats would risk lives for scant gain.

Kelpie shook his head, urgency tightening his voice “let’s make for the quarry” he urged Thurgan “there’s little useful knowledge we can bring to Raven’s Pass. But if we find a greater threat, be ready to ride and warn them” he met the ranger’s eye, making sure the point landed. Kelpie recognised that Thurgan was the swiftest among them, and the one most likely to slip through the woods unseen. Kelpie trudged onwards, guiding the caravan toward the only defensible ground they had.

Kelpie pushed onward, clinging to the hope that they would reach Blightstone Pit before the orcspawn found their trail. Orcs preferred to hunt at dusk or under full dark, that truth gave him a sliver of comfort, thin as it was. If they kept moving, they might just stay ahead of the danger.

The sounds of Melbor and Kaybar set his nerves on edge. The hounds wouldn’t be like that unless there was something was stirring in the woods “keep movin’” he urged the others, voice low but firm. They were far from safety, and every step mattered. The caravan had to reach the quarry and whatever slim refuge it might offer.

Ch 3 - Joining the Caravan to Raven's Pass

They were so close, Blightstone Pit just ahead, when Kelpie heard the forest erupt with noise. Orcspawn running from the treeline in a snarling tide, far more than Kelpie had feared. They had been caught in the open, and the creatures were many.

Kelpie’s heart lurched “faster!” he roared, desperation cracking through his voice as he urged the caravan onward. He shot a grim look toward Billworth “there’s too many of ’em, and we’ve still too far to go” he said “we’ll move quicker with one less wagon and the spoils from it might slow the orcs or split their numbers” it was a brutal choice, but a necessary one. Better to lose a wagon than lose lives.

Kelpie shifted his shield, breath tight in his chest, and pushed the mules harder. The old hedge knight’s instincts screamed that every heartbeat mattered now. They had to reach the quarry. They had to make their stand. And they had to do it before the orcspawn ran them down.

Ch 3 - Joining the Caravan to Raven's Pass

Kelpie ran with the others, breath burning in his chest, never once letting up his call to “keep movin’!” the orc spawn were behind them too many, too close and every heartbeat mattered.

When Jelb stopped to cast his spell, Kelpie almost halted beside him out of instinct. It took all his discipline not to. The wizard would catch up. He had to. The caravan was slow, but Jelb was quicker than he looked. Kelpie kept glancing back over his shoulder, until at last he saw the wizard sprinting to rejoin them. Only then did he let out the breath he’d been holding.

Also behind them, Billworth abandoned one of the wagons, cutting it loose. The hedge knight knew it was the right call. Between Jelb’s magic however brief and the discarded spoils, they had bought themselves precious moments. Would it be enough to reach the quarry? Kelpie pushed the caravan onward, refusing to let doubt slow his stride.

OOC Note Phantasmal Force ends as soon as the caster moves, so the illusion would vanish the moment Jelb ran to catch up. No rockslide just a group of sleeping orcs left behind. The time bought still stands, though, thanks to the spell’s initial effect and the abandoned wagon.

Ch 3 - Joining the Caravan to Raven's Pass

Relief washed through Kelpie as they finally reached Blightstone Pit, only for his heart to seize when he saw Urvan and Narinza were cut off, four more orcspawn bursting from the trees to bar their way.

Kelpie didn’t hesitate. He snatched up his sling, whirled it with practised speed, and loosed two stones in quick succession. Both shots flew wide, skittering harmlessly across the rocks. There was no time to curse his aim. The hedge knight dropped the sling and drew steel, planting himself to defend the caravan. His breath was ragged, his body bruised and bloodied from the long flight, but his stance was steady.

They were outnumbered. The woods still crawled with orcs. By day’s end, every one of them would be battered and bleeding. But here, at last, they had ground they could defend. Here, they had a chance. Kelpie raised his sword, jaw set, ready to spend the last of his strength to keep the caravan alive.
