Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

Soon, all the ingredients for the stew are bubbling over a fire in Nuanda's fireplace. The iron pot has a two-gallon capacity and is filled 3/4 of the way to the top. Nuanda has just diced upon onions, celery, and some tomatoes and added them and a sprig of garlic to the pot. She checks the baking bread, then sits in a rickety but comfortable looking rocking chair. "Another couple hours for the stew, but the bread will be ready soon and I have butter from Kirkilston." She gestures to Urvan, then points to a large flagstone, one of many forming her floor. Sir, would you be so kind as to lift that stone?" When the paladin does so the party sees a stone-lined rectangular column of open air going down beneath the floor perhaps a full ten feet. A rope tied to an iron hook six inches beneath the floor is obviously for the purpose of raising and lowering a wooden 2' x 2' platform. "A freshwater spring flows beneath the cottage. I use it to keep certain things cool: butter, pickled eggs, even sometimes leftovers. Would you be a dear and hoist that up so I can get the butter? Jelb, would you check the baking bread in the oven?"

Soon, the four of your are sitting and eating delicious sourdough bread slathered with cool, salted butter. You pass around a bottle of chilled wine (which Urvan also hoisted up from below), a vintage that Nuanda claims comes from a special friend. "I of course don't expect you to drink, Urvan. I understand your order's prohibition against alcohol, but perhaps Kelpie and Jelb would enjoy some fruit of the vine?"

Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

Urvan handled the bottle of wine gingerly, almost as if he were hesitant to touch it, but settled in for the meal with a polite smile and just a little too much formality. It was clear he did not know quite how to act with Nuanda despite their Witch Hunter's assurances, and so he defaulted to a sort of mild official posture, cordial but not exactly warm.

He nodded in agreement at Jelb's good foresight, but when it began time for dinner conversation he seemed to struggle a little as the bread was sliced up. "I'm afraid I have little news to share." he told her honestly. "I have the good fortune to be squired to a Sir Chevae Thrace and have been living in Eastdale in his service for some years." he explained.

"Where one might imagine tournaments or...tax collection when knighthood is mentioned, our duties are understandably more theocratic in nature, and I support Sir Thrace and the other Paladins where I can in duties that cannot be entrusted to a novitiate" he seemed to search for words for a brief moment. "Most of the reports he is called upon to investigate are merely youth dabbling with what they think may be the dark arts, or just trying to impress their peers or upset their parents, but there have been a few genuine cases that I have borne witness to during my training." he admitted before he asked in turn.

"You seem to have earned the respect of the locals...no mean feat." he told her before he began to eat.

Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

"Oh, I don't know about 'respect'; maybe a few actually feel that; for most the truth is probably closer to wariness. It is true there aren't many white witches. In fact, I know of only one other besides me."

Nuanda sips wine, then decides on another slice of bread. Outside, darkness is falling. "You Red Bannermen are right to go after black witches; they draw power from different sources, but always they are powers harmful to nature and bent upon destruction, domination. That is not the way. Demetra teaches that if we care for the world and work hard for its prosperity, it will in turn take care of each of us."

Nuanda notices Kelpie's suddenly sharpened interest. "Yes, I venerate Demetra. At one time, I sought to enter her priesthood, but the Prelate learned whose daughter I am, and that was that. He wouldn't hear of the daughter of Vinuvia joining any good-aligned religion in the Middenmark. I was judged for the sins of my mother."

She turns to regard Urvan. "I have met your liege, Sir Chevae. He came here six years ago, after my mother killed his younger brother, Fingal. He was certainly ... conflicted, but I must say he acted honorably. He explained that St. Ygg had revealed to him that I was unlike the other witches. Did you know he'd been sent to slay me, and did not carry out the order?"

Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

Jelb held his peace while Urvan and Nuanda talked.



When their conversation wound down, Jelb spoke of himself a bit.  "I've always had respect for the goodly gods that have held the line against the wicked and I believe most need laws to keep the morally challenged at bay.  But hidebound adherence to rules can lead to a bleakness all its own.  Where's the excitement if everything always goes as planned?  I seek a god who does what is good for others, but doesn't mind upsetting the applecart to wake people out of a stupor, or to test the righteous and proce them true... but who never causes harm to the innocent without bringing a blessing that exceeds it."

He hesitated for a long moment, then confided.  "And in my most insane dreams... if I can't find him, I will upset applecarts and bring joy and wakefulness to people until I become him."

Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

“Thank Demetra for this food” Kelpie murmured, offering a quiet prayer as the bread was shared in simple hospitality. Not wanting to seem discourteous, he accepted the wine as well. Toothless, he sucked at the bread and chewed slowly, content in the small pleasure of eating among friends.

He listened while Jelb, Nuanda, and Urvan spoke, nodding along as the conversation flowed. When the soup was ready, he slurped the broth with open delight. Once his belly was full, he wiped his chin with the back of his hand and smiled.

“Like my father before me, I travel the road in search o’ work” he said, settling into the tale as naturally as breathing “some call me sellsword… those more courteous say hedge knight” he left it to them to decide which fit.

“My father taught me that honour is a true man’s armour. He always helped those in need. Whether they could pay or not. Loyal to those he served, and grateful for every hearth that gave warmth. He thanked Demetra for the harvest, and for the good folk who tended it.”

Kelpie touched his brow, then his heart “my pa’s memory sits her and here. I try to honour it. I was his squire, and when he passed, I took up his sword to follow in his bootsteps” he stretched his legs beneath the table, easing the stiffness from sitting  “since then I’ve walked the road, fought when fightin’ was needed, laboured when work was required. A simple life but it’s the only one I’ve known these many years.”

Jelb listened and watched very carefully,  seeking fully to understand what Nuanda was doing, both in her wards and the words she was teaching them.  Well, as fully as an inexperienced mage of a different tradition and lineage could, anyways.

"Nevermind - got that part.  Will you be traveling in mind, body, or spirit, or will the revelations be coming to you by absorbing your attention?"  He asked instead.

Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

By the hearth, Kelpie felt safe, wrapped in warmth, good company, and the honest comfort of a roof overhead. But when he witnessed magic at work, a tingle crept down his spine. Magic was a strange thing to him: powerful, unpredictable, and far beyond his understanding. Best handled by others.

So when Jelb spoke the arcane words, Kelpie let out a quiet breath of relief. He would not need to remember them, nor attempt such things himself. He nodded along as the others discussed the spell’s meaning, not grasping the workings of it, but recognising its importance all the same.

The day’s weight settled on him then, and he decided it was best to rest while he could. Whatever tomorrow held, he would meet it with a clear head and steady hands. With the cot provided, he eased himself down. A bed, any bed, was a blessing. Far better than a hedge, and more comfort than he’d known in many a night.

Ch 2 - Supper With Nuanda

Urvan nodded a little, perhaps in thanks, at Jelbs deft repetition of Nuanda's instructions.

Not knowing the words themselves he privately felt that he could not in good faith repeat them, and he had already strived to be a polite guest in the service of hopefully finding the lost girl and returning her to her family.

The complexities of what their hostess were about to undertake were lost upon him, but he payed close attention all the same. It was his role, in a way, to learn these things so that he might find hints of them elsewhere in the form of the crafts darker mirror, and he focused upon absorbing and retaining this unique experience, one that most of his peers might never obtain.

Although his mentor had been here apparently, and on important business. That thought stayed with him even as he prepared to bed down for the evening.
