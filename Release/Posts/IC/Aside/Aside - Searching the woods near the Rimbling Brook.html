Searching the woods near the Rimbling Brook

The sight of the orcspawn tore a curse from Kelpie’s lips. Bandits were one thing, dangerous aye, but still men. Orcspawn were worse. There was savagery in them that chilled the blood.

“Back over the bridge!” he bellowed his companions “lest we be overrun and surrounded” they were outnumbered. The only hope was to force the enemy into a narrow choke where numbers meant nothing.

Kelpie held his ground until the others had crossed, then stepped onto the middle of the bridge, planting himself like a stone in the river. Here only one orc could reach him.

As warning and a challenge, he snatched his spear and hurled it overhand at the advancing orcs. The throw went wide, clattering uselessly into the brush.

He raised the horn to his lips and blew hard, the call echoing through the trees. A warning to the caravan. A summons to battle. And a promise that Kelpie, hedge knight and son of a hedge knight, would hold the line.

Searching the woods near the Rimbling Brook

The orc spawn were fearsome things, all brute strength and malice. Kelpie met the first blow head on, shield braced, boots planted. The strike crashed into him like a falling tree. It rattled his bones, split skin, and left him bruised and bloodied but not broken. He did not give an inch. Indomitable, he swung back, but the pain in his arm threw his aim wide and the blade hissed past the creature’s shoulder.

Jelb was right they could fight more freely off the bridge. But Kelpie knew the truth of it, his companions lacked the steel armour he wore. If he stepped aside too soon, the orc spawn would tear through them like wolves through sheep. He would retreat only when he must.

“Ready yourselves!” he barked to Geoffroy and Jelb, seeing more of the orcspawn splashing through the stream. His voice was rough, urgent, but steady. Kelpie tightened his grip on sword and shield, blood running warm down his arm, and held the centre of the bridge.
