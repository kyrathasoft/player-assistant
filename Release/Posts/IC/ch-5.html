Ch 5 - A Betentacled Escape

As they finally rested, Kelpie found his thoughts circling the horrors they had escaped. He wasn’t sure which was worse, the orcspawn that had hounded them through the woods, or the monstrous thing that dwelt in the depths of the mine. Either one could have ended them. Together, they had nearly done so.

The hedge knight stripped off his helm and sat down heavily, feeling every bruise and ache earned that day. None of them had been prepared for a battle like this “a difficult road yer uncle has chosen” he remarked to Thurgan, rubbing a hand across his brow “is it safe for us to rest the night here?” the question carried no fear, only the practical concern of a man who had survived too many ambushes to take quiet moments for granted.

To Geoffroy, he shook his head “there’s much smaller and safer prey we can hunt in the forest, praise be to Herne. Let us leave the mother o’ monsters in the pit where she belongs” Kelpie leaned back against the wagon wheel, sword across his knees, eyes still scanning the treeline even as exhaustion pulled at him. Rest was needed but vigilance would keep them alive.

She was not a tall woman, but wiry and with the look of a warrior... and a traveler, her boots and cloak dusty from the road.  Besides the shield she also wore plate armor -- old but fine, well cared for -- that gleamed faintly in the firelight.  A sword was sheathed at her side as well.  Yet despite this, she looked around at those gathered inside as though she was stepping into a room filled with old friends and neighbors.  Long, wavy red hair framed a kind face.

"This inn's reputation must be well deserved," she went on.  "Might I trouble you for a meal?  Stew, perhaps?" she asked Samuel, before turning back to the others.  Her hazel eyes glanced around the room once more, before pausing at the sight of Shade.  A thoughtful frown replaced the smile, though only for a moment.  "I've seen you before, sir, I believe," she ventured after a moment, questioningly.  "On the road, not long ago?  Neria Silverdale," she introduced herself, both to the drow and to the room at large.

Shade scratched his neck and peered at the red-headed knight. He recognized her straight away, what he was puzzling over was what odd coincidence had made her appear in his life once more. "On the road," he said after a beat. "Two, nearly three weeks ago. You helped me. Outside that village... Kirkilston, was it?"

He took in the others with a glance. "The men I mentioned? They overtook me on the road before I could cut into the forest. Only outriders, but I was hard pressed. It would have gone badly if not for the help of this woman." Shade stood, straining surreptitiously to make himself appear taller. "Well met, Neria Silverdale. The fight left no time for introductions. I am Shade, and I owe you my thanks." He bowed his head, then, suddenly self-conscious, he cleared his throat and sat, taking a hurried draft of the apple drink.

There must be something more than apples in this, he thought. Shade was seldom so forthright, preferring to remain in the background.
