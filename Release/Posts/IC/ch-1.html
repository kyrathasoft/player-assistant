Ch 1 - Kirkilston

Kirkilston is named after the church, or kirk, at its center. The church, alongside the Red Temple in Eastdale and the Watchtower in Westkeep, provide the three key centers of faith, administration, and governance in The Prelacy of Middenmark. However, unlike the more puritanical stance of the clerics in Eastdale, The Red Laws are more relaxed in Kirkilston and the immediate surrounding area.

Governed under Ecclesiastical Law of St. Ygg, Kirkliston is led by Petre, a seasoned Priest. The church is the centerpiece of the hamlet and, alongside the Red Temple in Eastdale and the Watchtower in Westkeep, forms a triad of religious and administrative authority in The Prelacy of Middenmark. The implementation of the Red Laws in Kirkliston is notably more lenient, reflecting the hamlet's more relaxed and less rigid approach to religious doctrine.

The hamlet's economy revolves around livestock, primarily sheep, and agriculture, with corn being the chief crop. Additionally, the (secret) production and export of mead contributes significantly to its local economy and trade, as does lumber production. Being a key stop on the caravan route, Kirkliston also benefits from trade and interactions with travelers and merchants.

Lacking a formal wall or significant defensive structures, the hamlet relies on a small group of brave, albeit untrained, men known as the Hamlet Watch. They are responsible for safeguarding the community against threats, particularly from the forces of chaos emerging from the nearby wilds. Their bravery is a testament to the rugged and resilient spirit of Kirkliston's inhabitants.

Daily life in Kirkliston (alternate spelling 'Kirkilston') is marked by a hardworking ethos, with most residents engaged in farming, shepherding, or local trades. The hamlet has a few essential amenities like a blacksmith, a tavern, and a small market area. Social life often revolves around the church and communal gatherings, with festivals and fairs being focal points of celebration and social cohesion.

Living in the shadow of the Blackpeak Mountains and the Darkforest, the people of Kirkliston are no strangers to hardship and the dangers posed by the wilderness. Their resilience, however, presents opportunities for adventurers and travelers, be it aiding in defense, exploring the nearby wilds, or engaging in the hamlet's modest but vital trade activities.

The jobs board at the center of the hamlet has the following postings:

  • caravan guards needed for a run to Raven's Pass - 2 days, pays 30 gp per day
  • a trio of dwarves is hiring teams to help reclaim the keep on the borderlands - 25 gp/day plus 5 gp per goblin tongue
  • Reglar, a ranger, is hiring huntsmen, loggers, and other stalwarts to help carve out a settlement/fort in Darkwood Forest
  • a mage staying in The Circle K inn is hiring explorers, claiming the Lost Caverrns of Archaia have been rediscovered

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

The roads of the Middenmark were never truly safe, but the Red Laws kept enough order that a lone traveller could still make their way most days, at least. Kelpie had little faith in the new gods, his beliefs were older, rooted in hedgerows, crossroads, and the quiet mutterings of common folk. Still, he respected the peace the Red Laws brought, and Kirkliston was a welcome sight after a long day’s march.

The notice board outside the square caught his eye. Guard duty on Raven’s Pass or throwing in with the settlers bound for the Darkwood Forest. Both paid. Both were dangerous. The choice could wait until morning.

For now, his feet carried him toward the warm glow of Morgan’s Morsels & Tankards. With only a single gold coin left to his name, Kelpie knew he’d be sleeping in the common room if the innkeeper allowed it.

He stepped inside, the smells of soup and warm bread wrapping around him like a blanket. Kelpie flashed a toothless grin at the innkeeper and raised his coin between two fingers.

“Good sir” he said, dipping his bald head in a polite nod “food and board, if it please you” he let the gold glint in the tavern light, proof enough he wasn’t begging “tell me do ye know when the caravan be passin’ through Raven’s Pass?” his voice carried the hopeful edge of a man eager for honest work and a heavier purse.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

Comfortably settled on the bench, Kelpie let his shoulders loosen for the first time that day. As he waited for his soup, he watched the evening crowd drift into the inn farmers with mud still on their boots, lumberjacks smelling of sap and sawdust, all carrying the honest scents of labour and open air. It was familiar, comforting. Kelpie smiled at each new arrival, offering a friendly wave to any who glanced his way.

The talk of a missing farmer’s daughter, though, soured the warmth in his chest. His expression tightened, and he turned his gaze toward the hearth. Bowing his head, he murmured a quiet prayer to Demetra, asking that the girl be found safe and returned to her family’s arms.

But prayer alone wouldn’t bring her home. Kelpie knew better than to rush off alone folly dressed up as bravery. What was needed was fellowship, a handful of capable souls willing to stand for the common folk.

He let his eyes wander across the room. The man beside him, the warrior by the window, even the knight with warhorse. Any one of them might lend their strength to such a cause.

Kelpie stroked his chin thoughtfully, the faintest spark of purpose kindling behind his toothless grin.

As the evening crowd began to filter into Morgan’s Morsels & Tankards from the surrounding hamlet and the gloom of dusk began to gather a lone figure made his way to within the light emanating through the windows, but no further. Within a few paces of the sign he lingered, looking a little lost, a little uncertain, almost like a moth attracted by the scents, sounds and glow of the tavern.

He stood out, like a sore thumb, or perhaps like a target given the red cross emblazoned upon the white tabard he wore. The chainmail hauberk beneath had been recently scrubbed and oiled, the cloying scent of it still lingered about him, while his kettle hat with a red braid round the brim was a size too large, making his pale and beardless features look that much more boyish in the light cast from the windows.

His smooth, fair features and downy blond hair stood in contrast to his above average height and strapping build, and in even starker contrast to the mace that hunt haft down from a brass ring at his belt, a few feet of stout hardwood capped by a small iron head with shallow flanges. A round shield with the same red cross design centered over the boss hung awkwardly angled over his pack, the fabric of his tabard bunched up beneath the trio of leather straps.

He could not be much more than twenty years at most, and few if any of those years had been spent in a laboring trade by the look of him, and many a look he did draw. If not to the emblazoned cross and weapon than to his handsome features, and if not to those than to his awkward and lingering presence outside Morgan's.

It was almost as if he was waiting for someone, but the minutes passed and some patrons had come and gone again in the time he had been out there, the sun now well set with the occasional bat flitting about the eaves over his head.

Where others might have baulked at such easy familiarity with a witch or with simple country folk Kelpie felt entirely at home. He’d earned his keep in many ways over the years, chopping wood, mending fences, hauling water, guarding doors, and sometimes even earning a few coins for his trouble. Hospitality was a language he understood.

He was quietly grateful that Nuanda planned to make a stew. Roast meat would have been a long, tedious affair for a man with no teeth; stew was a blessing. In the small cottage they would all be close together, working shoulder to shoulder. Kelpie gave his broad, toothless smile.

“Aye” he said, accepting his duty to cut the potatoes “give me a moment.”

He followed Nuanda outside, watching her draw water from the well as he began stripping off his armour piece by piece. The fresh air and the relief of shedding the weight made him sigh “the Darkwood… ’tis a dangerous place for a person alone” he remarked, half statement, half gentle question.

Once unburdened, he returned to the warmth of the cottage kitchen, ready to work alongside the others. He took up a knife and set to cutting potatoes with steady, deliberate strokes, content in the simple labour.
