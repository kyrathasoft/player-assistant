Ch 1 - Kirkilston

When the sky grew pale with the first light of morning, Kelpie was already prepared. Their talk the night before had made the route clear enough Nuanda the Good Witch, who lived only a short journey from Kirkliston. This would be but the first step, whatever news she shared would shape the road ahead.

“I will take the fore” Kelpie said as he stepped outside, the cool air waking him fully. He set off along the south eastern path leading out of the village “what may come, I will be your shield.”

It was not bravado, merely a vow spoken plain. A hedge knight he might be poorly equipped, penniless, and rough around the edges but he would stand between danger and those younger or more vulnerable than himself. That much he could offer.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

Once clear of the town, the forest pressed close around them dark trunks, tangled undergrowth, and the sense that anything might burst from the green at any moment. Kelpie kept his word and took the fore, meeting whatever challenge the Darkwood chose to throw their way.

When the boar came crashing through the brush, all fury and tusks, Kelpie planted his feet and held his ground. The beast’s charge faltered at the last moment, its tusks slashing wide as it thundered past him. Kelpie recovered quickly, shield snapping up as he brought his broadsword down in a heavy overhead arc. The blow was strong but just shy of true, the blade hissing past the boar’s bristled hide.

He grunted, dropped into a lower stance, and locked eyes with the creature steady, unflinching “Jelb, stay back” he warned, recognising how vulnerable his unarmoured companion was against such a beast.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

The melee ended as quickly as it had begun. The savage boar lay still, brought down by the dogs’ fury and the crushing weight of Urvan’s mace. Kelpie paused to catch his breath, then crouched beside the carcass. Up close, the beast was enormous far too heavy to haul any real distance.

Penniless as he was, Kelpie felt the tug of practicality. There was value here, if they were wise about it. But another thought pressed harder “we must press on to the good witch” he reminded the others “every moment wasted leaves poor Jenny in more peril.”

He stretched his shoulders, then set the tip of his sword against the boar’s flank “still… perhaps we take the best bits as a gift for Nuanda” he marked the line between shoulder and thigh with the flat of his blade “it’s not far, and we can manage this much between us.”

With a grunt, Kelpie lifted his sword and brought it down, severing the boar’s feet. He tossed them aside for the dogs “Melbor! Kaybar!” he called, letting the hounds feast while he looked to the others for agreement.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

As the adventurers crest a rise in the fog-laden forest, their eyes fall upon a crooked, moss-covered cottage that seems half-swallowed by brambles and twisted black branches. Its sagging roof is shingled with dark, weathered wood, and a thin column of smoke curls from a stone chimney, promising both warmth and a watchful inhabitant. Herbs, dried flowers, and small charms dangle from the eaves and across the doorway, interspersed with skulls and bones, giving the place an unsettling yet deliberate artistry. A narrow, muddy path winds past scattered feathers and wild lavender, leading to a low, crooked fence of spiked, blackened branches. On the porch and along rough-hewn tables, a blackthorn wand rests within reach, an iron dagger and bone-handled ritual knife lie beside bundles of dried herbs, and a small mirror of polished black glass catches the dim light. A witch-bottle containing hair, pins, and ash hangs by the threshold. Just within the doorway, a pale woman with dark, layered robes and sharp gray eyes peers out, her fingers streaked with ink and ash, exuding a calm, watchful presence. The whole scene suggests bargains struck in whispers, secrets kept, and knowledge both forbidden and potent, a home as alive with hidden power as its enigmatic occupant.

The woman speaks to her cat, though you're still too distant to hear her words; then the woman smiles at you from a distance, turns, and enters her cottage.

As you draw nearer, the black cat steps onto the game-path that leads to the cottage. It's green eyes shine in the morning sunlight that filters down through the forest eaves. He boldly approaches Kelpie then meows and rubs itself against one of the fighter's tough leather boots -- all the while checking out Jelb and Urvan.

After Kelpie tosses it a piece of meat, the cat meanders back toward the cozy cottage, seeming satisfied with both the snack and with the guests.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

Urvan lingered for a time perhaps fifty paces from the home and took the moment to sip water from his skin.

His recent childhood had been a world of straight lines and swept spaces, of the sound of low sonorous chants and the clear ring of a bell.

This moss covered cottage was an antithesis of sorts, all soft curves and dabbled greens and browns. It was not unpleasant, not at all really, but it was very, very different. He was not quite sure how to feel about it, and this made him uncomfortable.

As they neared his blue eyes roved over the baubles and curiosities, his hesitant curiosity plain as he had removed his kettle hat as they approached the home out of a sense of politeness.

"What a curious place..." Urvan half whispered, his tone wary and soft, as if he was still making up his mind.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

Where others might have baulked at such easy familiarity with a witch or with simple country folk Kelpie felt entirely at home. He’d earned his keep in many ways over the years, chopping wood, mending fences, hauling water, guarding doors, and sometimes even earning a few coins for his trouble. Hospitality was a language he understood.

He was quietly grateful that Nuanda planned to make a stew. Roast meat would have been a long, tedious affair for a man with no teeth; stew was a blessing. In the small cottage they would all be close together, working shoulder to shoulder. Kelpie gave his broad, toothless smile.

“Aye” he said, accepting his duty to cut the potatoes “give me a moment.”

He followed Nuanda outside, watching her draw water from the well as he began stripping off his armour piece by piece. The fresh air and the relief of shedding the weight made him sigh “the Darkwood… ’tis a dangerous place for a person alone” he remarked, half statement, half gentle question.

Once unburdened, he returned to the warmth of the cottage kitchen, ready to work alongside the others. He took up a knife and set to cutting potatoes with steady, deliberate strokes, content in the simple labour.

Ch 1 - Kirkilston

Settled into the simple rhythm of preparing a meal and freed of his armour Kelpie looked for all the world like any other farmer or village hand. Bald head, gummy smile, sleeves rolled up, he was entirely at ease. He worked steadily, cutting potatoes with the quiet contentment of a man who knew honest labour.

But at the reminder of why they were here, his mood shifted. His hands slowed, the knife resting a moment on the chopping board as he let out a soft sigh. Then, with a small nod to himself, he resumed his work.

“It’s the girl, Jelenneth… from the Heldrith farm” he said, voice low but steady. He shrugged, a gesture of simple truth rather than indifference “she’s been missin’ more’n a week now. We seek to return her home…” he paused, the knife stilling again for a heartbeat “…or at least let her family know what’s befallen her” pragmatic words, but spoken with a quiet weight that showed how deeply he meant them.
