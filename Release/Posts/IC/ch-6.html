Ch 6 - At The Mason's Apron

"What does it do?" Shade asked, looking at the cloth.

He had stared a long while into the dark liquid in his cup. Glancing only every now and then at the cloth everyone was discussing. Magic was something with which he was loosely familiar, in that intrinsic way common to the drow and most races of the Deep Kingdoms, but the apple drink had his mind drifting—home, his family, the shame he wrought, his disgrace—so it took longer than it should have for the conversation around him to seep through. And even longer for Neria's questions...

"What?" he asked, fixing his gaze on the red-headed paladin as her words finally stirred the mush of his attention. He went on slowly, pronouncing each word with a precision that didn't quite cover the small softening at their edges, "Red Tusk? Blanryde Hills? Hold." He raised his hand. "Are you saying Cromm is speaking to you about our mission?"

The evening had already been home to more revelations than Shade cared to count. First, a welcome he hadn't expected, even considering the sudden comradeship of the road; then a possible lead to Narinza and her fate, not to mention the mission between him and it; and now revelations from the gods? He was right to suspect more going on than simple coincidence. He pressed his fingers to his temple.

What mess have you gotten yourself into now? The tone was unmistakably his mother's. He shook it off.

"What does he want?"

Ch 6 - At The Mason's Apron

"Red Tusk..." Shade considered the name, then shook his head slowly. "I don't think I know that name."

For a moment, he had the sense of being washed along in events utterly beyond his control. Ever since fleeing his home, he'd scarcely had a moment to collect himself. It had been one long series of narrow escapes and dangerous flights, punctuated now and then by chance encounters. And few of those ended as well as that with Neria. That literal gods were taking an interest in his doings was profoundly disquieting.

He looked at the paladin, chewing his cheek. It must be the apple drink, he thought, but he had a sudden foreboding that their paths were inextricably entwined.

"Perhaps we will learn more as we go," he offered. "I have heard that gods are seldom clear about their wishes."

Shade slathered honey on another piece of bread and popped it in his mouth, washing it down with a generous glug of cider as he listened. The food of the surface was strange. Not bad—it was, in fact, very good—but strange. Back home (and he still thought of it that way), breakfast was often fish caught in underground rivers, mushrooms, and dense black bread. There were few sweet flavors, and no apples from which to make cider, either. Mushroom ale, moss tea. Nothing to compare to the lighter flavors of the surface.

He didn't miss them, but an occasional a piece of smoked eel would be a pleasant reminder.

"Good to meet you, Velorda," he said during the greetings, introducing himself in turn and recognizing Borca and Corba with a brief smile. His smile became a grin when Maximilian—apparently stung—volunteered his services to help train her. Never annoy a wizard, he thought; they are apt to turn you into a frog.

"We will be quite the party when we depart." And not hard to follow, if someone had a mind to it.
