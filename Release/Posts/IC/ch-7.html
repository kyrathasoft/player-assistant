Chapter 7 - Into the Red Thicket

Once the party ventured beneath the shade of the peculiar birch trees that made up Red Thicket, Shade lowered his hood and pulled down the mask that hid his nose and mouth. He still blinked against the sun. He was certain he would never get used to that: the bright light hanging above his head, burning down. It made his eyes water. As a habit, he chose to move at dusk and dawn, when the light was better suited to his sharp vision and not a positive bane. But his companions would likely not share his crepuscular habits.

"Elves," he said neutrally, looking at what were even to his eyes scarcely visible marks on nearby trees. Whether they were new or old, he couldn't tell. His feral cousins made signs in living bark that grew with time, their position signifying the age of the message, but he was no expert in the practices of lesser elves. What purpose these served was beyond his interpretation, but he guessed some kind of warding or blessing. Probably the former.

He touched his hand to the bark of the nearest birch, his skin a shadow on the white trunk. Old, but not ancient. These trees near the edges were practically youths compared to the old fathers he knew they would encounter further in. He took a breath and crouched on the path.

"The forest is alive; not just the individual trees. It has collective will and desire unlike any I have encountered, slow-formed though they would be by our standards." He recalled the monstrous fungal networks back home, the hypha extending for miles through rock and earth, like veins. What he felt here was akin to it, but not exactly the same. Fungi were all about decay and absorption. Saprophagy. He didn't feel that here. The Red Thicket oozed vitality and life. "I can't explain it, but offend one, offend all. We should avoid that. Keep our fires small and cut no living branches. There—" He pointed to a faint track on the ground. "Bear. Also deer, elk. The boots of bipeds, too. The path is well used by game and people. But the forest..." He stood, wiping his palms on his pants. "The forest is still wild. Its depths are beyond even my skill to know. We should expect large game, maybe even owlbears and perhaps even more unusual creatures. This is no common woodland."

"I can lead us, Kelpie." Wasn't as if he could possibly forget the cursed sun anyway. Heading west at least put it behind him for now. "I'm not advocating roping ourselves together, not yet, but we should stay close and note the person in front and behind you. You would be surprised how quickly you can become lost in a forest like this, even if nothing and nobody means you harm."

Shade took a breath, oriented himself, then broke trail through the undergrowth.
